Using and Reporting a Fit

Once the checks pass, the fit is a tool: for predicting a reading, estimating a constant, or computing something from the coefficients. Each use needs its own kind of error bar.

This module covers the two bands every fit comes with, how to carry the fit's uncertainty into a number you compute from it, and what to write down. All data is simulated from y = 1 + 2x with sensor noise SD 2 unless a trial says otherwise.

What to take away

    
    
      
  1. Two bands, two questions. "Where's the true line?" uses the confidence band. "Where will the next reading land?" uses the prediction band, which never shrinks below the sensor's noise.
    2. 
      
  2. Both bands assume the model still holds. Outside the tested range, they're a best case.
    3. 
      
  3. Derived results need the full covariance. Slope and intercept are correlated; use the delta method or a bootstrap.
    4. 
      
  4. Report the residual SD in units, ranges on every number, and what you checked. R² alone isn't a result (Module 1).
    5. 
    

    
The series in one line each

    

      
Modules 1–2
Look at the data and the residuals; R² can't tell good from bad.

      
Modules 3–4
Uneven scatter and linked errors leave the line alone but break the error bars.

      
Module 5
Noise in x and hidden drift bias the line itself, invisibly. Design the experiment to prevent it.
